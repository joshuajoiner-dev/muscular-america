# 03 — Editorial Principles

**Status:** APPROVED (founding principles; examples may expand)  
**Authority:** Controls voice, certainty, and ethical framing across publications

## Purpose

Set the editorial posture for Muscular America content: clarity, usefulness, and honest certainty.

## What belongs here

- Voice and certainty principles
- Language boundaries (shame, hype, false precision)
- Evidence posture at the editorial level

## What does not belong here

- Exact citation formats (see Reference and Citation Standard)
- Layout and type specs
- Per-publication architecture details

## Governing principles

1. **Clarity before cleverness.** Prefer the sentence that lands.
2. **Usefulness before volume.** Short and usable beats long and impressive.
3. **Observation before instruction.** Show what is happening before telling people what to do.
4. **Principles before trends.** Durable ideas outrank novelty cycles.
5. **Accessible language without condescension.** Explain without talking down.
6. **Explain rationale without overexplaining.** Enough why to act; not a lecture by default.
7. **Avoid shame-based fitness language.** No moralizing bodies or effort.
8. **Avoid false certainty.** Do not dress estimates as laws.
9. **Distinguish evidence, convention, experience, and opinion.** Readers deserve the difference.
10. **Respect different bodies, abilities, goals, and experience levels.** Defaults are not universals.

## Certainty language

Prefer:

- “Often,” “commonly,” “many trainees find,” “a useful heuristic”
- Explicit labels for convention vs research vs observation

Avoid:

- Absolute medical claims
- Universal prescriptions from personal anecdote
- “Always / never” unless truly warranted and classified

## Identity note

Editorial principles may share operational DNA with other projects under the same Founder. Public language, metaphors, and brand voice for Muscular America must remain independent.

## Publication-specific tone

Each publication may refine tone (Field Guide: compact and browsable; Simply Fit: TBD) without violating these principles.
