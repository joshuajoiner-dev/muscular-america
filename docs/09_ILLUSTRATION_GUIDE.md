# 09 — Illustration Guide

**Status:** FRAMEWORK ONLY — no commissioned style lock  
**Authority:** Boundaries and use cases for future art

## Purpose

Set responsible illustration standards for training content before assets are produced.

## What belongs here

- Illustration categories
- Anatomical and inclusive representation standards
- Boundaries against diagnostic or shame framing

## What does not belong here

- Final art boards
- Stock photo shopping lists as doctrine
- Medical imaging guidelines beyond “don’t fake diagnosis”

## Illustration categories

- Exercise diagrams
- Equipment illustrations
- Body-position diagrams
- Instructional sequences
- Environmental gym scenes
- Icons
- Calculator diagrams
- Print illustrations

## Standards

1. **Anatomical responsibility.** Simplify for clarity; do not invent biomechanics.
2. **Clarity over spectacle.** If the move is unclear, the drawing failed.
3. **Inclusive representation.** Vary bodies, ages, and abilities across the library over time. Do not make one exaggerated physique the universal default.
4. **No medical-diagnostic imagery** as a rhetorical trick.
5. **Avoid unnecessary before-and-after framing.** Transformation theater is not the default teaching tool.
6. **Distinguish observation from prescription.** Captions should not overclaim.

## Captions and alternatives

Every informative illustration needs:

- A clear caption or adjacent explanation
- Alt text that communicates the instructional point
- Evidence/classification notes when the image implies a normative claim

## Relationship to Field Guide

Field Guide art should support quick scanning — simple, segmented, readable at small sizes — not dense textbook plates by default.

## Change process

Style lock requires Founder review, specimens, and an Editorial Decision.
