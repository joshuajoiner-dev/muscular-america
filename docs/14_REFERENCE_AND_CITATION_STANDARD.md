# 14 — Reference and Citation Standard

**Status:** APPROVED (classification framework)  
**Authority:** How claims are labeled and sourced

## Purpose

Prevent false certainty by requiring evidence classification and usable citations.

## What belongs here

- Evidence classifications
- Citation expectations
- Rules for mixing research, convention, and observation

## What does not belong here

- A full bibliography for every draft
- Pseudo-citations invented to look scientific

## Evidence classifications

| Classification | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Established consensus | Broadly accepted across reputable professional sources |
| Supported research | Backed by studies worth citing; not necessarily settled forever |
| Emerging evidence | Early or mixed findings; handle cautiously |
| Professional convention | Common practice among coaches/clinicians without claiming law |
| Coaching practice | Practical method used in training settings |
| Founder observation | First-person or editorial observation; clearly labeled |
| Illustrative example | Example for clarity; not proof |

## Editorial rule

Do **not** present all statements with equal certainty.  
If a Field Guide entry mixes classes, label the load-bearing claim.

## Citation practice (initial)

- Store source records under `content/references/`
- Entries link by reference IDs
- Prefer primary or high-quality secondary sources
- Include enough detail for a reader to find the source later
- Calculators must note formula provenance when applicable

## Forbidden moves

- Citing a source that was not consulted
- Dressing Founder observation as established consensus
- Using before/after anecdotes as clinical evidence

## Relationship to calculators and illustrations

Calculator assumptions and instructional diagrams that imply norms must carry an appropriate classification or disclaimer.
